Predictive maintenance is a fusion problem: RAG + time-series

By Malek Haroud· 2026-11-23· 4 min read

A bearing temperature trend will tell you that something is failing. The service manual, the work-order history and the failure reports will tell you what to do about it. Predictive maintenance needs both.

The two data worlds

Structured sensor data is the domain of time-series: anomaly detection, trend analysis and remaining-useful-life estimates. Unstructured knowledge — manuals, inspection checklists, failure modes, past work orders — is the domain of retrieval-augmented generation. RAG turns that corpus into a queryable, cited knowledge base.

Why RAG, and not just an LLM?

A plain LLM answers from memory and cannot cite the page it came from. In maintenance — especially GMP-regulated maintenance — that is a non-starter. RAG retrieves the relevant passages first, then generates from them, with traceable sources. The result is an answer you can audit.

Fusing the signals

The two worlds meet in the maintenance workflow: the time-series raises the alert, the RAG layer proposes the diagnosis and the procedure, and the technician acts with the source document in hand. The business outcome is fewer unplanned stoppages and a leaner MRO inventory — the stated target is a measurable ROI within 90 days.

Size the RAG before you build it

The first design decision is sizing: how much corpus, which chunking, which embedding model, which retrieval budget. That is the job of RAG-Architect, the companion tool in Smart Maintenance — dimension the pipeline first, then build it.

Smart Maintenance is deployed as a live showcase at smartmaintenance.helveticore.com.
