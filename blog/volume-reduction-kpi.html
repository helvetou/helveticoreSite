96.07 % less data at the edge — how we measured it

By Malek Haroud· 2026-11-23· 4 min read

A KPI is only real when a script, the raw results and a short report all live in the repository. Here is the measurement behind EdgeStream-GW’s headline number.

The target

Reduce the northbound (cloud-bound) data volume by at least 75 %, while preserving the information the cloud actually needs. Volume is measured as UTF-8 JSON bytes of the normalized Frame stream.

The scenario

A deterministic simulator models 50 energy meters polled at 1 Hz for 300 seconds — 15 000 raw frames. Each frame flows through the Modbus RTU adapter into a per-meter WindowAggregator with a 60-second RMS window.

The aggregation collapses 60 frames per meter into one frame that carries, per quantity, the representative value plus .min and .max. That is the whole trick: many frames in, one frame out.

The result

  • Frames: 15 000 → 250 (98.33 %)
  • Measurements: 60 000 → 3 000 (95.00 %)
  • JSON bytes: 6 666 214 → 262 004 (96.07 %)

Notice the byte reduction is slightly lower than the frame reduction: each aggregated frame carries three numbers per quantity (value + min + max), not one. That is the honest cost of keeping the trend.

Why determinism matters

The simulator is seeded, so the same run always produces the same readings. The measurement was repeated across five seeds: 96.07 % every time. A KPI you can re-run and reproduce is evidence; a number you can only claim once is marketing.

The lesson generalizes: measure at the boundary you actually pay for (the bytes you send), not the internal stage that looks most impressive.
